In the 2021 Career Quest Engineering talk, I used a small diagram that started
with only two words: will and way. Then it became motivation and ability. Then a
third point appeared: narrative.

That third point is the one that still interests me. Motivation and ability are
easy to treat as the whole career system. Do I want to do the work? Can I do the
work? If both answers are yes, the path should move. But careers are lived
across time, and time breaks simple diagrams. Past work has to be understood.
Current work has to make sense. Future work has to be imaginable. That is the
job of narrative.

The talk preserved the path plainly: video editor, then project manager, then
software engineer. The video editor chapter was about helping people in need and
creating digital artifacts that got more people involved. The project manager
chapter carried hard work, leadership, confidence, and support for sales people,
but also the lesson that money as the primary motivation did not work for me.
The software engineer chapter named the motivation most directly: I love to
code. It also carried forward the previous abilities, including creating clarity
from ambiguity.

## Motivation Answers Why

Motivation is the reason the work is worth doing. It is not always noble or
polished. Sometimes it is service. Sometimes it is craft. Sometimes it is money,
security, recognition, curiosity, or the need to leave a previous kind of work
behind.

The problem is that a motivation can work for one chapter and fail in the next.
Helping people in need made sense in the video editor chapter. Better pay looked
like a possible answer during the project manager chapter. Loving code became
the clearer answer in the software chapter. None of those observations requires
turning the whole path into destiny. They show that motivation has to be checked
against the work as it is actually lived.

That is close to the argument in
[Find Your Why: Mastery, Autonomy, and Purpose](/blog/find-your-why-intrinsic-motivation/):
when work goes flat, the question is not only whether to try harder. It is which
part of the why has gone quiet. Motivation is not a slogan. It is an operating
signal.

## Ability Answers How

Ability is the set of things a person can actually bring to the work. The useful
part is that ability is portable in less obvious ways than job titles suggest.
Creating digital artifacts did not vanish when the medium changed. Hard work,
leadership, confidence, and support did not disappear between roles. Creating
clarity from ambiguity did not start from nothing when software engineering
arrived.

This is where a career change can be misread. From outside, it may look like a
break. From inside, it can be a transfer of abilities into a new medium. The
medium changes from footage and timelines, to project coordination, to code and
systems. The person still carries learned habits of making, communicating,
supporting, and improving.

Ability alone is not enough. A person can be capable and still misaligned. That
was the lesson of the project manager chapter as stated in the talk. Work can
use your strengths and still fail to satisfy the reason you are there. That is
not a failure of ability. It is a mismatch between the how and the why.

## Narrative Keeps the Path Intelligible

Narrative connects the parts that motivation and ability cannot connect by
themselves. The talk used three time frames: Back[Story] as past, Story as
current learnings, and User[Story] as future. That framing is a little playful,
but it makes a serious point. A career needs a way to carry memory forward.

Without narrative, the past becomes either a mistake to hide or a set of random
facts. The video editor is erased by the engineer. The project manager becomes a
detour. The person has to pretend the current role was always obvious. That is a
thin story, and it wastes useful material.

With narrative, the path can stay honest without becoming overdetermined. The
past explains available abilities. The current chapter explains what is being
learned now. The future chapter asks what the person wants to do again, improve,
or stop repeating. Narrative is not branding. It is the maintenance of
coherence.

The triangle fails when one point is missing. Motivation without ability becomes
wishful. Ability without motivation becomes hollow competence. Motivation and
ability without narrative become a pile of disconnected chapters. A career does
not need a perfect story, but it does need enough story for the person living it
to understand why the next step belongs to the same life.
