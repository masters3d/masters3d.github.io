I went looking through everything I have written here for what I had actually
said about signal-to-noise ratio, expecting to find a post about it. There is no
such post. The word "noise" shows up three times in the whole body of work, and
every single time it plays the same small part: noise is the pile you throw
away. The local read of an engineer
[treated as noise, not signal](/blog/independent-fire/). Metrics that
[signal real problems versus noise](/blog/quest-engine-building-high-agency-teams/).
A word that makes every turn of the wheel [noisy](/blog/word-impact-at-work/)
even though you still move.

Three mentions, one job. Noise is always the discard pile, and it is always
discarded by somebody. That last part is the one I want to pull on, because it
is the part the phrase hides. We talk about signal-to-noise as if it were a
property of the environment, like humidity. It is not. Noise is a verdict, and
on an engineering team somebody with authority issues it.

## Noise Is a Verdict, Not a Property

In the original engineering sense, signal-to-noise is a ratio measured at the
receiver. The same transmission is clean on one radio and unusable on another,
because what counts as noise depends on the instrument doing the listening and
on what it was tuned to expect. Nothing in the signal itself announces which
half is which. The receiver decides.

Carry that over to a team and the uncomfortable part shows up immediately. When
a lead says "that is just noise," they are not reporting a measurement. They are
making a call, with their own vantage point as the instrument. The engineer who
raised the concern transmitted. The receiver classified. And the classification
is not neutral, because the receiver's tuning was set by their own history: the
failures they personally lived through, the kinds of risk they have been burned
by, the vocabulary they happen to share with the person speaking.

This is the same failure I described in
[Independent Fire](/blog/independent-fire/) from the other direction. There I
said centralized command fails when the people at the edge can see more than the
center can and the system gives them no authority to act on it. The
signal-to-noise framing adds the mechanism underneath: before anything can be
acted on, it has to be _received_, and a center that is the sole receiver is
also the sole judge of what was noise. The British officer did not ignore the
soldier behind the oak out of malice. He never classified the soldier's view as
information in the first place. The formation had no channel for it, so it
arrived, if it arrived at all, as chatter.

## The Dial You Reach For Says a Lot

If the ratio is measured at the receiver, there are only three things you can do
to improve it, and which one a team reaches for first tells you most of what you
need to know about that team.

You can **amplify the signal**: ask people to make their concerns louder,
sharper, better written, escalated through the right channel. This is the one
most organizations default to, because it costs the receiver nothing. It also
quietly makes volume a prerequisite for being right, which selects for the
people most comfortable being loud rather than the people closest to the
problem.

You can **suppress the noise**: fewer meetings, fewer channels, fewer people in
the room, a tighter filter on what reaches the center. Sometimes this is exactly
correct. The risk is that filters are built from yesterday's failures, so a
filter tuned until the center is comfortable is a filter that will reliably drop
the one class of problem nobody has been burned by yet.

Or you can **improve the receiver**: widen who is allowed to be a receiver at
all. This is the expensive option and the only one that scales, because it stops
treating the center as the single antenna. A team where ten people can each hear
and act on their own slice of the ground does not need every transmission to
survive the trip to the middle. That is the same multiplier Independent Fire is
about, except the thing being distributed is not the authority to fire, it is
the authority to decide what counted as information.

The tell is which dial gets reached for by reflex. Teams that reach for
amplification have a listening problem they have labeled a communication
problem. Teams that reach for suppression have usually confused calm with
health.

## Low Signal and Wrong Signal Are Different Failures

Noise is not one thing, and collapsing it into one label does the same damage
that collapsing a release into
["bad"](/blog/a-case-against-using-the-word-bad-in-engineering/) does to an
incident. A version is not good or bad in the abstract, it is a distribution of
outcomes across a population. The discard pile has a distribution too, and it is
worth naming the parts.

There is **low-amplitude signal**: the correct read, delivered quietly, by
someone without the standing to be loud. Nothing is wrong with the content. It
just arrived under the threshold.

There is **unshared vocabulary**: a real concern phrased in terms the receiver
does not parse, which gets heard as vagueness. This is the one that
[shared understanding work](/blog/story-mapping-shared-understanding/) exists to
fix, and it is why "I could not follow what they were saying" is a statement
about a channel, not about a person.

There is **genuine noise**: restatement, speculation with no ground under it,
the habit of adding a comment to be visible. This exists. Pretending it does not
is its own kind of dishonesty, and a team that refuses to ever filter will drown
just as surely as a team that filters everything.

And there is **inconvenient signal**: the clear, well-stated, correctly aimed
concern that would cost something to act on. Of the four, this is the one most
likely to be labeled noise, and it is the only one where the label is a decision
rather than a mistake. Three of these are fixed by better instruments. The
fourth is fixed by honesty about what you are choosing not to hear.

## The Career Version Nobody Warns You About

Here is where this stops being a team topic and becomes a personal one.

The work that is hardest to perceive as signal is prevention work. I made this
argument about the word "impact" already: teams give more credit to the person
who fixes a visible failure than to the person who
[prevented the failure in the first place](/blog/word-impact-at-work/). In
signal terms, a prevented outage transmits nothing. The entire evidence of the
work is an absence. The dramatic rescue, by contrast, is a transmission so loud
that no receiver can miss it.

[Glue work](/blog/minimize-humans-as-glue/) has the same shape. The person
holding the team together generates enormous value and almost no signal, because
the output of glue work is other people's output. So does the slow, compounding
kind of progress: early learning, foundational design, system cleanup, all of
which look quiet for a long time before the curve bends.

I do not think the answer is to go be louder. The answer I have actually landed
on, over nine years of writing things down, is to **make the absence legible**.
Keep the record yourself. Not for performance reasons, but because an absence
with a written history attached stops being an absence and becomes a trace: this
is the failure class we stopped seeing after the change, this is the question
that got asked in March that saved the rewrite in August. You cannot raise the
amplitude of something that did not happen. You can leave evidence that you were
the reason it did not.

And the second half of that answer is to notice when you are the receiver.
Everyone is somebody's center. Every time I have caught myself thinking "this is
noise" about a teammate's concern, the honest follow-up question has been: noise
to me, from here, with what I currently know. That is a statement about my
instrument.

## Private Notes Are the Unfiltered Channel

The reason I keep coming back to [notes](/blog/nine-years-of-copious-notes/) is
that a private log is the one channel with the filter switched off. Nothing in
it has to justify its existence to a receiver. The half-formed observation, the
thing that felt wrong in a meeting, the concern I could not yet phrase well
enough to say out loud: all of it gets written down at full width, and nobody
grades it on the way in.

That matters more than it sounds, because almost everything that later turned
out to be important signal started its life looking like noise to me. The
pattern you can name on the third occurrence was invisible on the first. A
filter applied at capture time discards exactly that material, because at
capture time it has not accumulated enough weight to look like anything. This is
also why the [background brain](/blog/the-background-brain-boredom-makes-ideas/)
needs something to work on: it is doing pattern extraction over the stuff you
kept, and it can only find the signal in what you did not throw away.

So the rule I have ended up with is asymmetric. Capture wide, filter late.
Transmit narrow, but keep the unfiltered record. The notes are where I am
allowed to be noisy, which is what makes it possible to be precise everywhere
else.

## The Setting

If I had to compress all of this into one line, it would be that **a team's
signal-to-noise ratio is not a condition it inherits, it is a setting someone
chose**, usually without noticing they were choosing.

Every filter is a bet about which category of information is safe to drop. Every
"that is just noise" is a receiver describing itself. And the leader's actual
job here is not to be a better filter. It is to stop being the only receiver in
the room, so the local read of the person closest to the ground never has to
survive a trip to the center to count as information.
