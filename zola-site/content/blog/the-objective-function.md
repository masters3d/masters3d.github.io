Every Quest Engine post so far has pointed at the objective function and then
walked past it. [The WHY post](/blog/quest-engine-the-why/) calls it the layer
that sits above the operational cycle.
[Find Your Why](/blog/find-your-why-intrinsic-motivation/) calls it the thing
your intrinsic motivation is a personal version of. The
[Matz post](/blog/joy-freedom-motivation-matz-quest-engine/) files "the
objective" into it and moves on. It has been load-bearing in every one of those
arguments and has never had a page of its own.

This is that page. Four things to settle: what an objective function actually
is, what the general human one is, how the self-referencing structure nests
without going circular, and where the Search force hides when you reduce the
whole framework to "the why and the how."

## What an Objective Function Is

An objective function is **the rule a system uses to decide what counts as
better.** Not the goal. The rule that ranks goals.

In machine learning it is literally a function: feed it a candidate outcome, it
returns a score, and the whole apparatus climbs that score. The reason it is
worth borrowing the term rather than just saying "goals" is that the machine
learning version comes with a warning attached. A model does not pursue what you
meant. It pursues what you scored. If the score is a bad proxy for what you
wanted, the model will optimize hard and arrive somewhere you did not want to
go, and it will do it efficiently, and nothing in the system will object.

Teams work the same way. Every team has an objective function whether anyone
wrote it down or not, because the moment work gets prioritized, something is
being ranked. When nobody states the rule explicitly, the team silently adopts
whatever is easiest to measure: tickets closed, hours logged, lines shipped,
meetings attended. Those are proxies. They are not wrong, exactly, but nobody
chose them, and unchosen proxies are how a group of competent people end up two
years deep into work that nobody can explain the point of.

Two words worth keeping apart, because the framework uses both:

- The **objective** is a particular state of the world you want to produce: a
  working site, a shipped feature, a problem that stops being a problem.
- The **objective function** is the standing rule that decides which objectives
  are worth having in the first place, and tells you when one has stopped being
  worth it.

An objective is a value. An objective function is the thing that assigns values.
You can hit your objective and still be failing your objective function, which
is exactly what "we shipped it and it changed nothing" feels like.

## The General Human Objective Function: Explore, Play, Joy

Here is the claim this post exists to make plainly: **the general human
objective function is Explore, Play, and Joy.**

Those three are the subject of
[Discovery, Play, Joy](/blog/secular-meaning-of-life/), where I framed them as a
secular account of meaning. What I did not say there, and should have, is that
they are not merely a nice description of a full life. They are the scoring
rule. When a person asks "is this worth doing?" and answers honestly rather than
strategically, these three are what the honest answer is made of:

**Explore** (Discovery) scores novelty and understanding. Does this take me
somewhere I have not been, or show me something I did not know? This is the
before-the-act component, the searching force, the thing
[Mastery](/blog/find-your-why-intrinsic-motivation/) is made of.

**Play** scores agency. Is this mine to shape, and does shaping it produce
interesting results? This is the during-the-act component, the driving force,
the felt form of Autonomy.

**Joy** scores connection. When this is done, will it have mattered to someone,
including me? This is the after-the-act component, the renewing force, the felt
form of Purpose.

The reason to call these the _general_ objective function is that they are
terminal. Ask why you want any specific thing and you get a chain: I want this
feature shipped, because the team needs the release, because the customer is
blocked, because... Keep pulling and the chain always lands on one of these
three, and then stops. Nobody can answer "why do you want to understand things?"
with something more fundamental than wanting to understand things. **These three
are where the regress ends, which is exactly the property an objective function
needs.** A scoring rule that needed its own scoring rule would never terminate.

Your particular objective function is this general one with weights on it. Some
people run heavy on Explore and will trade a great deal of comfort for a
question they have not seen before. Some run heavy on Play and will take a
smaller problem they fully own over a larger one they do not. Some run heavy on
Joy and need to see the person on the other end before anything feels worth
doing. The weights are personal, they move over a lifetime, and most of the
confusion people have about their own motivation is a weighting they never
examined.

Two practical consequences fall out of this immediately.

**Organizations do not share your objective function, and that is normal.** A
company's function scores revenue, risk, and retention. Yours scores Explore,
Play, and Joy. Motivation is not a feeling that visits you; it is the measure of
how much those two rules overlap on the work in front of you. Where they
overlap, effort is free. Where they do not, you are running on
[extrinsic fuel](/blog/find-your-why-intrinsic-motivation/) and will have to
refill it from outside, continuously, forever. Stated that way, "I am
unmotivated" stops being a character flaw and becomes a measurable misalignment
with a short list of possible causes.

**Attaching your score to an implementation detail is a trap.** If your Play
score is tied to typing code by hand rather than to shaping systems, then every
change in tooling reads as a loss, because it is one. Keep the score on the
thing that survives the medium.

## The Self-Referencing Structure, Made Explicit

The Quest Engine is described as
[self-similar](/blog/quest-engine-introduction/), which is accurate and also the
part most likely to feel like hand-waving. Let me make it precise, because "it
is recursive" is only useful if you can say what recurses, what the base case
is, and why it is not circular.

**What recurses is the triad, not the content.** At every level you find the
same three-beat shape: a before (search for what better means), a during (drive
toward it with ownership), and an after (renew by checking whether it was the
right target). That shape holds for a single code review, a project, a career,
and a life. It holds for the operational loop and it holds for the layer above
the operational loop. The content at each level is completely different; only
the shape repeats.

**The levels connect through one specific handoff.** This is the piece that was
missing. Each level's objective function is produced by the Renew step of the
level above it.

- Life-level Renew asks what kind of life this is adding up to, and what it
  outputs is the objective function for your career.
- Career-level Renew asks whether this direction is still yours, and what it
  outputs is the objective function for this year's project.
- Project-level Renew asks whether this is still the right thing to build, and
  what it outputs is the objective function for this week's task.

Which is why the layer above the operational loop is not a different kind of
thing from the loop. **It is the same loop, running slower.** Strategy is
retrospection at a longer wavelength. The reason it feels like a separate layer
is that its cycle time is measured in quarters and years, so from inside a
two-week sprint it looks stationary, like a fixed sky rather than the slow
weather it is.

**The base case is Explore, Play, Joy.** The regress has to stop somewhere or
the structure is circular, and it stops at the three terminal scores. Everything
above them is derived; they are not derived from anything. That is what makes
this a well-founded recursion instead of a loop that justifies itself.

**The recursion runs both ways, and the upward direction is the whole point.**
Objectives flow down from Renew to the level below. Evidence flows back up:
executing reveals whether the objective was any good, and that evidence is the
raw material of the slower Renew above. A system that only sends objectives down
is a bureaucracy. A system that only sends evidence up is drift. Both directions
running is what makes the thing compound, and it is the precise sense in which
[the HOW feeds back to refine the WHY](/blog/quest-engine-introduction/).

## Where the Search Went: Why, How, and the Missing What

Now the question that prompted this post. Reduce the framework to the two words
people actually use, Why and How, and run them through the timing rule that
settles every other placement in this framework
([Search is before, Drive is during, Renew is after](/blog/search-mastery-drive-autonomy-renew-purpose/)):

- **Why** is a backward-looking alignment check. "Why are we doing this" judges
  a target after it has been set. That is **Renew**.
- **How** is the mechanics of doing it. That is **Drive**.

Two of three. **The Search force is missing from the Why/How pair, and the
missing word is What.**

What is the before-the-act force. It is the question that produces the target
rather than mechanizing it or auditing it: what should exist, what would better
even look like here, what is the actual shape of a good answer. Skip it and you
get the two most common failure modes in engineering, both of which look like
competence from a distance. Why without What is a beautifully argued business
case that never specifies a thing to build. How without What is a team executing
at high velocity against a target nobody chose, which is just the unchosen-proxy
problem again, moving fast.

This also explains a collision in the vocabulary that has been quietly confusing
things. "The WHY" in [the WHY post](/blog/quest-engine-the-why/) means the whole
objective function layer above the loop, all three forces of it. "The why" in
[the Why doc and the How doc](/blog/why-doc-how-doc-steering-agents-from-the-middle/)
means one force inside a layer: the renewing, purpose-facing one. Same word, two
different altitudes, which is exactly the kind of thing a self-similar structure
will do to your vocabulary if you let it. The fix is to say which level you are
on, and to stop treating Why and How as a complete pair, because they are a
two-legged stool.

Here is the whole stack in one table.

| **Question** | **Force** | **Human motivation** | **Felt as** | **When it acts** | **In practice**                      |
| ------------ | --------- | -------------------- | ----------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| **What**     | Search    | Mastery              | Explore     | Before the act   | What should exist? What is "better"? |
| **How**      | Drive     | Autonomy             | Play        | During the act   | Who decides? How does it get built?  |
| **Why**      | Renew     | Purpose              | Joy         | After the act    | Did it matter? Does it still?        |

Read the columns and every triad in the series lines up: Search/Drive/Renew,
Mastery/Autonomy/Purpose, Explore/Play/Joy, and now What/How/Why. They are not
four frameworks. They are one structure described from four distances: the
mechanism, the motivation, the feeling, and the question you would actually ask
out loud.

The conventional order "why, what, how" is the order you _present_ things in,
because you lead with meaning when persuading people. The order the framework
runs in is **What, How, Why**: find the target, own the work, check the target.
Why comes last not because it matters least but because it is the audit, and an
audit that happens before the work is a prediction, not an audit.

## Using It

The objective function is a diagnostic, like everything else here. Three
questions, in cycle order:

**What am I actually optimizing for, and did I choose it?** If the honest answer
is a proxy you inherited (ticket count, hours, output volume), that is the
Search gap, and it is upstream of every other problem on this list. Nothing
below this line can be fixed by working harder.

**Which of my three scores is going unpaid?** Explore flat means the work has
stopped teaching you. Play flat means you have responsibility without ownership.
Joy flat means you have lost sight of who it is for. The one that is flat tells
you which negotiation to open, and they are different negotiations.

**Where do my function and the work's function actually overlap?** Not "do they
align," which is a yes-or-no question with a depressing answer. Where, and how
much. The overlap is where effort is free, and most of the useful career moves I
have seen were someone deliberately enlarging it rather than switching jobs and
hoping.

What makes this the most durable layer in the framework is that it is the one
agents do not touch. An agent can search, draft, and refactor; it can do a great
deal of the How. It cannot tell you what counts as better, because that is a
question about what you value, and the agent has no stake in the answer. **As
more of the How gets delegated, the objective function stops being the abstract
part of the job and becomes most of it.** Explore, Play, Joy are what is left
when the typing goes away, and they were always the thing being optimized.
