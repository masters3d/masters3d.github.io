This one is about the others. There are three steps, each with its own post, and
this is the high-level map of how they fit together plus the two properties that
make the arrangement work at all. It deliberately does not go into the detail of
any one step. It is the post about how to read the other three.

## The Three Steps

**[Why: the Primary Directive.](/blog/the-objective-function/)** The standing
order that everything downstream is ranked against. It comes in two nested
forms: a specific one that is personal and particular to your situation, and a
general one that is structural and shared (Explore, Play, Joy). Answered first.

**[Who, What, When, Where: the Contextual Core.](/blog/the-four-ws/)** The four
questions that locate you against the directive. Four separate questions, four
separate ways to be lost, bounded by the directive so that the search actually
terminates. Answered second.

**[How: the Action Phase.](/blog/quest-engine-introduction/)** The execution,
the driving force, the part where the work gets done and the evidence gets
produced. Last, and smaller than it usually is, because most of what makes
execution hard was decided in the two steps above it.

Then it cycles. The evidence How produced is what the directive gets audited
against, and the audit issues the next directive.

The ordering is the whole practical payload: **Why → Who/What/When/Where → How →
Why.** It runs against the default, which is to start at How, because How is
where the typing is and typing feels like progress. Everything else here is an
argument for that order, or an explanation of why the order is harder to keep
straight than it looks.

## The First Meta Property: It Is Self-Referencing

The three steps are not three different kinds of activity. They are one shape,
and the shape shows up again inside each of its own parts.

Take the Contextual Core, which looks like a single step from the outside. Start
actually doing it and it unfolds into the same three: you need a directive for
the search (what am I trying to locate, and when is it enough), you need to
locate yourself within the search (who knows this, where is it written, when do
I need it by), and then you need to execute the search. Why, then the four Ws,
then How, one level down, nested inside a step that was itself the four Ws.

The same is true of the Action Phase, which has its own directive (what does
done mean here), its own locating (what does this code touch), and its own
execution. And it is true going up: a career is one cycle whose How is a decade
of projects.

Two things follow, and they are the reason this property is worth naming instead
of just admiring.

**The shape is what recurses, not the content.** Nothing about a code review
resembles a career. The three-beat shape of both is identical. When this
framework seems to claim that everything is everything, that is the claim being
made badly: only the structure repeats.

**Levels connect through a single handoff.** Each level's directive is the
output of the audit one level up. Life-level Why issues the career-level
directive; career-level Why issues the project-level directive; and so on down.
Which means the layer "above" the loop is not a different kind of thing from the
loop. It is the same loop running slower, and it only looks stationary because
its cycle time is measured in years.

The recursion has to stop somewhere or it is circular rather than nested. It
stops at the general directive: Explore, Play, Joy are terminal, which is to say
nothing underneath them explains them. That is the base case, and it is what
makes this a well-founded structure instead of a set of ideas justifying each
other in a ring.

## The Second Meta Property: It Is a Loop

The steps are not a pipeline with an end. The audit at the close of one cycle is
the directive at the open of the next, which is the same event described from
two sides. That single identity is what makes the whole thing compound rather
than merely repeat.

Information moves in both directions, and both directions are load-bearing:

- **Downward: directives.** Each level hands the level below it a rule for what
  counts as better. A structure with only this direction is a bureaucracy:
  orders with no evidence that they are working.
- **Upward: evidence.** Executing reveals whether the directive was any good,
  and that evidence is the raw material of the slower audit above. A structure
  with only this direction is drift: lots of learning, no direction to apply it
  to.

Both running at once is the compounding. Each cycle improves what you do, and
also improves what you are optimizing for.

This is also the honest answer to a reasonable objection: if Why comes first,
how do you ever answer it the very first time, with no evidence? You do not, not
well. The first directive is a guess. **The loop is what upgrades a guess into a
directive**, and expecting the first pass to be correct is a misreading of what
kind of structure this is.

## The Consequence: Half the Vocabulary Is Relative

Here is the part that trips people up, including me, and the reason this post
exists rather than being a paragraph in another one.

Once the structure is both self-referencing and a loop, a lot of the words used
to describe it stop being properties of things and become **positions relative
to wherever you are currently standing.** "Before" is not a property of the
search. It is where the search sits relative to the act you happen to be
considering. Change which act you are considering and the labels move, even
though nothing about the structure changed.

The clearest case: the four Ws are described as the "before" step, because they
come before the action. But when you sit down and actually do the locating work,
that work is now the current activity. It has its own before (its directive) and
its own after (did I find what I needed). The step that was "before" is now the
"during." Nothing moved except the frame.

So the terms split cleanly into two kinds, and keeping them apart resolves most
of the confusion:

**Fixed terms.** These do not move. They mean the same thing at every level and
from every vantage point.

| Term                       | What it is                                          |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Why / Primary Directive    | The rule that ranks; always answered first          |
| Who, What, When, Where     | The four locating questions; always the middle      |
| How                        | The execution; always the last step of a cycle      |
| Search, Drive, Renew       | The three forces, in that order                     |
| Mastery, Autonomy, Purpose | The motivations attached to those forces            |
| Explore, Play, Joy         | The general directive; terminal, same at all levels |
| The order itself           | Why → four Ws → How → Why                           |

**Relative terms.** These are coordinates, not properties. Each one is only
meaningful once you say which act and which level you are standing in.

| Term                    | Relative to what           | What moves it                                |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Before / during / after | The act under discussion   | Changing which act you mean                  |
| Current                 | Whatever you are doing now | Starting the next step                       |
| Above / below           | The level under discussion | Zooming out to a slower cycle, or in         |
| Faster / slower         | The neighbouring level     | Which two levels you are comparing           |
| First / last            | One cycle                  | Where you cut the loop (it has no true end)  |
| The why                 | Altitude                   | Whether you mean the whole layer or one step |

That last row deserves its own sentence, because it is the single worst
ambiguity in this whole series. **"The WHY" sometimes means the entire layer
above the operational loop, all three forces of it, and sometimes means one
specific step: the directive.** Both usages are in the posts. Neither is wrong.
They are different altitudes, and a self-similar structure will do this to your
vocabulary every time if you let it.

The practical rule is short: **when using a relative term, say the frame.** Not
"the search comes before," but "the search comes before the action." Not "the
why sits above," but "the why sits above this cycle." It is one extra clause and
it removes the entire class of confusion, because the ambiguity was never in the
idea, only in the sentence.

## How to Read the Other Three

So the series reads like this. Start with
[the directive](/blog/the-objective-function/), because without it the next step
has no edges. Then [the four Ws](/blog/the-four-ws/), because without them the
action is executing against knowable unknowns. Then
[the action](/blog/quest-engine-introduction/), which is smaller and better
posed than it would otherwise have been. Then back to the directive, carrying
evidence.

And read all three knowing the two properties: the same shape is inside each of
them, and the end of the cycle is the beginning of it. Those two ideas are not
decoration on the model. They are what makes it a model rather than a list of
three good habits. A list of steps tells you what to do next. A structure that
is self-referencing and closed tells you what to do next **at any scale, from
any starting point, including the one where you are already halfway through and
do not know which step you are in.**

Which, in practice, is most of the time. The useful question is rarely "what is
the first step." It is "which step am I currently standing in, and did I skip
the one before it?" That question only has an answer because the structure
loops, and it has the same answer at every scale because the structure repeats.
